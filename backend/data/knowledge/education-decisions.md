## Education Decision Statistics

### Earnings Premium
- Bachelor's degree holders earn 70% more than high school graduates over a lifetime: $78,400 vs $49,400 median annual (Georgetown Center on Education and the Workforce 2025)
- STEM degree median earnings: $98,000/year at mid-career (Georgetown CEW 2025)
- Graduate degree premium over bachelor's: 20-25% in most fields, but varies dramatically — MBA median $115K, Masters in Social Work median $58K (BLS 2025)
- ROI varies by field: engineering/CS degrees pay back within 5-7 years; humanities/arts degrees may take 15-20 years to break even on total costs (Georgetown CEW ROI analysis 2025)

### The Cost Side
- Average student debt for bachelor's: $37,800 (Federal Reserve 2025)
- Average student debt for master's: $71,000; for professional degrees (MBA, JD, MD): $120,000-$200,000 (Education Data Initiative 2025)
- Monthly payment on $100K at 6.5%: ~$1,150/month for 10 years
- Opportunity cost of 2-4 years out of the workforce: $100K-$300K in lost earnings depending on current salary

### Completion and Outcomes
- 6-year bachelor's degree completion rate: 63% at 4-year institutions (NCES 2025)
- Graduate program completion: 50-60% for PhD programs, 85%+ for professional master's (Council of Graduate Schools 2025)
- Unemployment rate: 2.9% for bachelor's holders vs 6.2% for high school diploma (BLS 2025)
- Career changers who add credentials earn 29% more in their new role than those who switch without upskilling (Coursera 2025)

### What This Means For Decision-Making
Education is a financial investment with real numbers: the earnings premium is significant and well-documented, but so is the debt burden and opportunity cost. The ROI depends heavily on the specific field, institution cost, and current earning potential. For career changers, targeted credentials (certificates, bootcamps, specific master's) often deliver better ROI than broad degrees. The 37% non-completion rate for bachelor's degrees means commitment and fit matter as much as the decision to enroll.
