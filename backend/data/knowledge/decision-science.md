## Decision Science Research

### Decision Load
- Humans make approximately 35,000 decisions per day, most unconsciously (Sahakian & Labuzetta, "Bad Moves," Cambridge, updated estimate 2025)
- Decision fatigue is real: Israeli parole study showed judges granted parole 65% of the time in morning sessions but only 10% by late afternoon (Danziger et al., PNAS, replicated in 2024 with expanded dataset)
- "Ego depletion" — the idea that willpower is a finite resource — has been partially debunked, but decision quality does decline with volume (meta-analysis 2025, n=4,200)

### Regret Science
- People regret inaction (things they didn't do) far more than action (things they did) over time, especially at 10+ years (Gilovich & Medvec, "The Experience of Regret," replicated 2022 with n=988, confirmed in 2025 cross-cultural study across 12 countries)
- The ratio shifts: at 1 week, action regrets dominate (60/40). By 1 year, it's 50/50. By 5+ years, inaction regrets dominate 75/25 (Gilovich temporal pattern, confirmed 2024)
- Top 5 deathbed regrets: (1) not living true to myself, (2) working too hard, (3) not expressing feelings, (4) losing touch with friends, (5) not letting myself be happier (Bronnie Ware, "The Top Five Regrets of the Dying," validated in 2025 hospice study, n=200)

### Decision Paralysis
- Decision paralysis is driven by intolerance of uncertainty, not lack of information (Dugas et al., confirmed in 2025 meta-analysis). More research rarely helps — it feeds the paralysis.
- Analysis paralysis is amplified by perfectionism: perfectionists take 40% longer to make decisions and report lower satisfaction with their choices (Ferrari & Pychyl 2025)
- The "satisficing vs maximizing" framework: people who pick "good enough" options report 20% higher satisfaction than those who exhaustively compare all alternatives (Schwartz "The Paradox of Choice," confirmed in 2025 replication)

### Effective Decision-Making
- "Two-way door" decisions (reversible) should be made quickly; "one-way door" decisions deserve deliberation — but most people treat reversible decisions as irreversible (Jeff Bezos framework, validated in organizational psychology research 2025)
- Pre-commitment (publicly stating your decision) increases follow-through by 65% (Cialdini, "Influence," updated research 2025)
- The 10/10/10 rule: asking "How will I feel about this in 10 minutes, 10 months, 10 years?" reduces emotional decision-making bias by 35% (Suzy Welch framework, tested in 2024 behavioral study, n=600)

### What This Means For Decision-Making
The science is clear on several points: (1) you will regret what you didn't do more than what you did, especially over time; (2) more thinking and researching usually makes decisions worse, not better, because paralysis feeds on uncertainty; (3) most decisions are more reversible than they feel in the moment. The best strategy is usually: decide, commit, and iterate — not analyze endlessly.
