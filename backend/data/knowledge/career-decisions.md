## Career Change Statistics

### Success Rates and Satisfaction
- 67% of career changers report better overall job satisfaction after the switch (Keevee 2025 survey, n=2,400)
- Only 13% of people who want to switch careers actually follow through (Resume-Now 2025)
- Average salary increase for career switchers: 5.2% in year one; 15-20% by year three if the new field has higher market value (BLS Occupational Outlook 2025)
- 6.3% occupational transfer rate annually in the US (Bureau of Labor Statistics 2025)

### Timeline and Execution
- Successful career transitions take 1-2 years from decision to stable new role (Harvard Business Review 2024)
- The "identity gap" — the psychological discomfort of being a beginner again — peaks at months 3-6 and is the #1 reason people abort career changes (Herminia Ibarra, "Working Identity," confirmed in 2025 follow-up research)
- 72% of career changers use their existing network, not job boards, to land their new role (LinkedIn Workforce Report 2025)

### Financial Reality
- Average time to match previous salary in a new career: 18 months for lateral moves, 3-5 years for complete industry changes (PayScale 2025)
- Career changers who upskill before switching earn 29% more in their new role than those who switch first and learn on the job (Coursera Global Skills Report 2025)

### What This Means For Decision-Making
The data shows career changes work for most people who actually commit — but the 87% dropout rate reveals that the psychological cost of transition (identity gap, beginner status, temporary income dip) is what stops people, not the outcomes. The ones who succeed typically leverage existing relationships, accept a temporary step back, and give themselves 18+ months to reach stability.
